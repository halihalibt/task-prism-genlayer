Delivery integrity: key terms and background

SHA-256 is a hash function. A hash is a fixed-length fingerprint of bytes.
A manifest is a document that lists hashes. A delivery manifest binds a URL
to a SHA-256 hash of the exact response bytes. UTF-8 is a text encoding.
Bytes are the raw content returned by a server. A mismatch means two hashes
are not equal. A verdict is a decision. Scores measure quality. FINALIZED is
a transaction state. A read-back is reading state after finalization. A
read-back of the latest-final state is the newest finalized state. Limitations
are things a system cannot do. Examples show how something works. Steps are
ordered actions. Verification is checking something. Consensus is agreement.
Validators validate. Integrity means unchanged. Public means anyone can fetch
it. Checking a manifest means verifying it somehow. This page covers delivery
manifests, hashes, SHA-256, UTF-8 bytes, mismatches, verdicts, scores,
FINALIZED states, latest-final read-backs, examples, limitations, and steps.
